Pax Buttonica · hardware

Flasher

Plug an Atom into this laptop with a data cable, find out what it is, and put the latest hub or button firmware on it. Nothing to install: the browser does the flashing. Chrome or Edge only. Close any other tab that has the hub connected first.

1 · What's plugged in

Looking…
Every Atom you've allowed this page to use before is asked who it is, with no popup: a button answers with its id and blinks white, a hub answers as a hub, a brand-new Atom says nothing (factory firmware). The popup only appears for an Atom you haven't allowed yet, once.

2a · Make it a hub

The one that stays on the laptop's USB. Only one of these.

This browser can't flash; use Chrome or Edge.

2b · Make it a button

The ones in the shells, with a key and a hex. Same firmware on every button; they tell themselves apart by their own ids.

This browser can't flash; use Chrome or Edge.

3 · Over the air

Buttons on firmware 0.3.0 or later never need the cable again: with the hub on this laptop, this lists every button the hub hears, with its version, signal and when it was last heard, and sends the published image through the hub to any that are behind, one at a time; each reboots into it, its light filling blue as the image lands. Presses show in the log. Close the game tab first; it holds the hub.

How it goes

  1. Plug the Atom in. The first time, click Allow another Atom and pick it in the popup; after that it's recognised on its own. The list above says what's on it now.
  2. Click Connect under hub or button and pick it in the flasher's popup (that one is the flashing tool's own and can't be skipped; with two Atoms plugged in, the list above tells you which is which by unplugging one), choose Install, wait for "Installation complete" (about 20 seconds), close it.
  3. Click Ask again. It should now answer with the new version. Buttons blink white while they answer.
  4. Do the hub first after a firmware change, then each button. A button flashed before its hub only reaches it by luck.
  5. Once a button is on 0.3.0 or later, leave it in its shell: later versions go to it over the air, from section 3.
If the install fails to connect, unplug and replug the Atom and try again; if it says the port is busy, another tab has it. The terminal alternative is firmware/flash.sh hub or firmware/flash.sh button.
