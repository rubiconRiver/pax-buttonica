The cap on a bought switch

The same flat cap and the hex riding inside it, but nothing printed bends and nothing hangs inside the cap. Like the Printables dome: the cap is closed underneath by a floor with three legs, and sits on an M5 Key Unit, a keyboard switch in a Grove case. Its spring returns the cap, its click is the click, its own end stop takes the slap. The printed parts only guide. The cell and the Rider lie side by side on the lid as in v2; the Atom hangs under the platform above the Rider. Drawn round the real parts and checked at rest and pressed. Not printed yet.

What you are looking at

The cap hangs on the housing's lip. A floor closes it from below; the hex stands on three short posts (a small stand glued to the floor), three legs go down from it into hollow bosses on the platform, and its middle rests on the keycap. Press anywhere on the cap: the keycap goes down 4 mm, the switch clicks at about 2 and stops at 4, and the whole thing comes back on the switch's own spring. The legs are the guide; the floor landing on the bosses is the backstop.

Size
72 × 80 mm, 62 mm tall to the cap (v2: 50). The cap stands 17.5 above the lip
Press
4 mm on a keyboard switch; the switch is the stop
Print
Seven parts: cap, floor and stand (transparent), platform, cradle, housing, lid. All new, none needs supports
Needs
Nothing new: a Key Unit, the Grove 1-to-3 splitter, two M3 screws
Feel
A keyboard key the size of your palm. A heavier clicky switch (Kailh Box Navy or Jade) drops in if the blue one is too light

Parts

To find out in a print: whether the blue switch lifts the cap, hex and floor (about 35 g against about 35 g of spring), and whether the hex's ear holes are where the three posts expect them. Sizes marked MEASURE in make_shell_v6.py: the switch's travel and click point, the splitter's footprint.
