# Large dome ("massive") arcade push buttons, 60 mm to 100 mm, as a replacement for the 3D-printed slap button

Source-type tags used below: **[MFR]** maker's own page or datasheet; **[DATASHEET]** switch maker datasheet; **[SELLER]** retailer listing (often copied text, treat as a claim); **[COMMUNITY]** builder blog, wiki or forum; **[LOW]** a weak or AI-written source, kept only because nothing better turned up. Prices are as of 2026-10-01. A price marked USD came from a US or overseas shop. Where no CAD price was found, the gap says so.

Research ran 2026-10-01. Amazon.com, Amazon.ca and Solarbotics product pages would not load for the fetch tool, so their details come from search-result snippets only. That is flagged where used.

## 1. Which large dome buttons exist, and which are well regarded?

### Takeaway
At 100 mm almost everything on the market is one of two lineages. One is **Sanwa OBSA-100UMQ**, the Japanese arcade original used in Konami's pop'n music cabinets: about USD 30 to 51 each, often sold out, and sold without a lamp. The other is a **generic Chinese 100 mm dome** sold under many names: Adafruit "Massive Arcade Button", SparkFun "Big Dome Pushbutton", Abra PBS-100MM, EG Starts, Arcity, Easyget, XINWANG, PMD Way, and the "game buzzer" listings. The Korean **Samducksa CWB 401** and the Chinese **Baolian** brand sit between the two and are the ones rhythm-game builders call better than the generic ones. At 60 mm only generic parts turned up (Adafruit 60 mm, EG Starts 60 mm, Arcade Express 60 mm). I found no 60 to 100 mm dome from Industrias Lorenzo, Suzo-Happ or Seimitsu.

### Inferences
- The Adafruit, SparkFun, Abra, EG Starts, Arcity, PMD Way and "buzzer" 100 mm buttons share the same specs (100 mm cap, 24 mm screw-in body, about 70 mm deep, 3-terminal microswitch, "10,000,000 cycles" text, 12 V lamp). They are very likely the same Chinese factory part with different branding. Expect the same feel from all of them. The real choices are generic vs Samducksa/Baolian vs Sanwa.
- For a "way, way better" feel, the only 100 mm part with an arcade-maker pedigree and a named switch is the Sanwa OBSA-100UMQ. Samducksa CWB 401 is the mid-price arcade-grade option.

### Gaps
- I found no Seimitsu button of 60 mm or larger. Seimitsu PS-14-KN looks like a standard ~30 mm button, but I could not confirm Seimitsu's large-button range from a primary source.

## 2. Mechanical specs: dome, hole, panel, depth, travel, force, switch, rated life

### Takeaway
All the 100 mm domes found mount in a **24 mm hole** and hang about **70 to 72 mm below the panel**. That is a tall, round body, very different from the current 56 mm cap over an AtomS3. The 60 mm generics need a **28 to 30 mm hole** and about 52 to 67 mm of depth. Switches are 3-terminal SPDT microswitches with 4.8 mm (.187") switch spades and 6.3 mm (.25") lamp spades. The only **datasheet-backed** life number is for the Omron V-10 used in Sanwa SD-cabinet buttons: **50,000,000 mechanical operations**. Every "10,000,000 cycles" figure on generic listings is unsourced seller text. Cherry/ZF D44X, a common drop-in replacement, is quoted at 10M+ by sellers.

### Cited Findings
**Generic 100 mm (Adafruit / SparkFun / Abra / EG Starts family)**
- SparkFun COM-09181: dome 100 mm, **mounting hole 24 mm**, depth or height **72 mm**, weight 100 g. It has a three-terminal microswitch "tested to withstand 10,000,000 cycles" that "can be easily replaced", and a 12 VDC LED with a removable bulb holder. "Remove the middle ring to drop the button's overall height." [SELLER] — [SparkFun](https://www.sparkfun.com/big-dome-pushbutton-red.html)
- Abra PBS-100MM: cap 100 mm, **overall height 70 mm, mounting hole 24 mm**, "Durable nylon material". Microswitch is "max 3A @ 120 VAC", "reliability tested to 10,000,000 cycles", 3 terminals. Described as having "a great tactile, 'clicky' feel". [SELLER] — [Abra PBS-100MM-GN](https://abra-electronics.com/electromechanical/switches/arcade-game-switches/pbs-100mm-gn.html)
- Adafruit 1185: 100 mm dome, "10mm deep plastic base", "common arcade microswitch", normally open, 0.187" switch terminals and 0.25" LED terminals. Adafruit lists no hole size or panel thickness. [SELLER] — [Adafruit 1185](https://www.adafruit.com/product/1185)

[SELLER/LOW] — [Focus Attack D44X](https://focusattack.com/d44x-75g-187-microswitch/); [Paradise Arcade D44X](https://paradisearcadeshop.com/products/cherry-pushbutton-microswitch); [Alibaba Cherry guide](https://electronics.alibaba.com/buyingguides/cherry-micro-switch-guide-d44x-vs-d45x-vs-e-series)

### Inferences
- **Travel:** The Adafruit 60 mm cap drops from 15.2 mm to 12 mm, so it travels about **3 mm** (derived from Adafruit's numbers). No 100 mm listing states travel; the same lamp-holder and microswitch design suggests a similar 3 to 5 mm.
- **Depth is the big integration change.** A 100 mm dome needs about 70 mm of clearance under a 24 mm hole, and the electronics would sit beside or under the switch, not under a flat cap. The SparkFun "remove the middle ring" trick lowers it somewhat.
- **Electrical fit is trivial.** Every one of these is a plain SPDT microswitch. Wire COM to GND and NO to the ESP32 GPIO with the internal pull-up. The stock 12 V lamp is irrelevant if replaced with WS2812s.
- **Life:** a TI game is maybe a few hundred presses per button. Even a 1M-cycle switch outlives the project. The 10M claims do not need to be true, but the 50M Omron V-10 figure is the only verified one. If durability matters most, buy any 100 mm dome and swap in an Omron V-10/V-15 or a Cherry/ZF D44X. All share the standard 3-terminal footprint; verify the fit in the specific holder.

### Gaps
- No datasheet was found for Sanwa's LHS1F or MS-O-3R switch, so no life figure is sourced for them.
- No actuation force is published for the generic 100 mm domes, apart from builders' measurements (section 3).
- Panel thickness range: no listing for any 100 mm dome gave a maximum panel thickness. The body is threaded and nut-secured, so it presumably suits typical 3 to 19 mm arcade panels, but that is unverified.
- No 100 mm listing gives plunger travel.

## 3. What makes them feel good or bad; reported failure modes

### Takeaway
Generic 100 mm domes are criticised for **very heavy springs and stiff switches**: rhythm-game builders cite 500 to 600 g total. They can feel **mushy** when a light switch has to move the big cap. The best-documented fixes are removing or replacing the spring and swapping the microswitch. Sanwa's buttons are praised for a "crisp sound", but new ones "may initially stick" until worn in. I found no slap-test or off-centre wobble reports for any 100 mm dome. "Never sticks when slapped off-centre" remains unproven for all of them.

### Cited Findings
- Generic 100 mm domes come with "extremely heavy springs and switches (upwards of 500-600g total)", and builders should "Consider budgeting for new springs and switches." [COMMUNITY] — [rhythm-cons.wiki 100 mm](https://rhythm-cons.wiki/parts/buttons/100-circular-dome-buttons/)
- On XINWANG 100 mm clones, the springs were "extremely stiff" and the microswitches "rather stiff", measured at "around 130g of force". "The official arcade buttons are apparently around 100g total to press." [COMMUNITY] — [macoy.me](https://macoy.me/blog/diy/PopnCon)
- The same builder removed the springs and let the microswitch return the cap; then "the buttons feel quite good". Pairing a feather (~30 g) Zippy switch with a hand-wound spring lost the click: "the clickiness from the micro-switch is lost due to the weight of the button and spring", making it "a bit mushy". [COMMUNITY] — [macoy.me](https://macoy.me/blog/diy/PopnCon)
- Springs come out by pushing the button all the way down, pinching the tabs, and pulling the plunger out of the stem. [COMMUNITY] — [macoy.me](https://macoy.me/blog/diy/PopnCon) (via search summary)
- Sanwa pop'n sets deliver "a crisp sound when pressed". New buttons "may initially stick": use the heavier 400 g springs until they wear in, then move to 100 g springs. [SELLER] — [Bluespring Express](https://bluespringexpress.net/en-us/products/popn-music-sanwa-button-set-obsa-100umq)
- Seller claims for the generic 100 mm: "great tactile, 'clicky' feel" (Abra); "immediate, heavy 'click'" and "built for heavy hands and repeated use" (SparkFun). [SELLER] — [Abra](https://abra-electronics.com/electromechanical/switches/arcade-game-switches/pbs-100mm-gn.html); [SparkFun](https://www.sparkfun.com/big-dome-pushbutton-red.html)
- The BYOAC FAQ says leaf-switch buttons are "generally accepted as the better feeling button, but … harder to maintain". Microswitch buttons have "a slightly stiffer feel" but are "less prone to dead button syndrome" and click on every press. [COMMUNITY] — [BYOAC FAQ - Controls](https://arcadecontrols.com/arcade_controls.shtml)
- A builder drilling a cable hole in a 60 mm button body found "the plastic of the button cracks easily". [COMMUNITY] — [hackaday.io, Lex Kravitz](https://hackaday.io/project/174102/log/181863-hacking-an-arcade-button-to-insert-a-neopixel-ring)
- An AI-written buyer's guide lists wobble or rattle from poorly cut holes as a common issue. Its "customer feedback" quotes ("loud, crisp click", "LED stays bright after 6 months") are unattributed. [LOW] — [Alibaba 100mm guide](https://electronics.alibaba.com/buyingguides/100mm-dome-push-button-guide-how-to-choose-right)

### Inferences
- For a slapped and held button, the heavy generic spring plus a stiff switch is probably fine, and maybe even desirable: a slap has plenty of force. The concern is feel, not function. If the stock feel is too heavy, the documented tune is a lighter spring (or none) plus a quality microswitch (Omron V-10/V-15 or ZF D44X).
- A 100 mm cap riding on a ~24 mm plunger tube has a long lever arm. An edge slap tilts the plunger in its bore, so off-centre binding is plausible on cheap parts. The Sanwa's "initially stick" note suggests even the premium part can bind until worn in. This needs a hands-on test: buy one generic and one Sanwa or Samducksa, then edge-slap them.
- Hold for ~1 s is a non-issue for any microswitch button. It stays closed while held.

### Gaps
- No arcade-controls.com, r/fightsticks, r/arcade or r/cade thread specifically on 100 mm dome sticking, cracking or wobble turned up in the searches. Amazon reviews could not be loaded.
- No cycle-life or abuse testing of any 100 mm dome was found. All life numbers are switch ratings, not whole-button ratings.
- No source describes how the dome is guided (bushing length, plunger material) for any brand. Abra says "durable nylon"; nothing else was found.

## 4. Lighting: translucency, stock lamp, and replacing it with WS2812s

### Takeaway
Every big dome ships with a **single lamp**: a 12 V LED with a built-in resistor on 6.3 mm spades (some 5 V versions), or a 14 V #194 wedge bulb on Sanwa, often sold without a lamp. None ships with addressable RGB. Adafruit itself warns the 100 mm and 60 mm "doesn't illuminate the entire button face evenly" because the cap is shallow. Builders have put **NeoPixel rings** inside 60 mm domes: an 8-LED ring hot-glued into a ~45 mm cavity only a few mm deep, with wires fed through the old lamp-clip slots. 3D-printed 4-contact replacement lamp sockets exist on Thingiverse. I found no published build putting NeoPixels in a 100 mm dome, though Instructables has a "Neopixel Dome Button" page I could not read.

### Cited Findings
- Adafruit 100 mm: "an optional LED included that will light up the button. The LED has a built in resistor so you can run it up to 12V but given the massive size and shallow depth of the button, it doesn't illuminate the entire button face evenly." [SELLER] — [Adafruit 1185 PDF](https://mm.digikey.com/Volume0/opasdata/d220001/medias/docus/2550/1185_Web.pdf)
- Adafruit 60 mm carries the same uneven-illumination warning ("due to shallow depth"). [SELLER] — [Adafruit 1190](https://www.adafruit.com/product/1190)
- SparkFun 100 mm: "12VDC LED indicator light with removable bulb holder". [SELLER] — [SparkFun](https://www.sparkfun.com/big-dome-pushbutton-red.html)
- PMD Way 100 mm: "Illumination requires 5V DC". Easyget sells a "5v 100mm" version; an unbranded listing offers 5 V or 12 V. [SELLER] — [PMD Way](https://pmdway.com/products/huge-100mm-illuminated-round-dome-arcade-button); [Easyget](https://www.amazon.com/Easyget-Shaped-Illuminated-Self-resetting-Projects/dp/B00XRC9URW); [unbranded](https://www.amazon.com/Button-Illuminated-Microswitch-Arcade-Machine/dp/B0GDPVWM7D)

- Adafruit's Learn guides that use the 100 mm button (music control, timers, adaptive controllers, the "Ninja timer") use its stock LED. None found swaps in NeoPixels. [MFR guide] — [Adafruit 1185 related guides](https://www.adafruit.com/product/1185); [Ninja timer: wire the buttons](https://learn.adafruit.com/ninja-timer-giant-7-segment-display/wire-the-buttons)

### Inferences
- **The current NeoHEX** is 31 mm across flats and ~36 mm with ears. That is under the ~45 mm cavity diameter documented in a 60 mm cap, so it fits by width. It would sit only "a few millimetres" under the dome, though, which means hot-spots. In a 100 mm dome there is more width but the depth is still shallow (Adafruit: "10mm deep plastic base"). A ring, such as a 16- or 24-LED ring, or the hex board plus a printed diffuser, would spread light better than one central lamp. Expect a bright ring or centre with dimmer edges unless a diffuser is added.
- **Mount the LEDs on the moving cap or on the fixed body?** Builders glue the ring into the cap, so it moves with each press and the wires flex every slap; the fine, flexible silicone wire matters here. Mounting the LEDs on the fixed body, under the plunger, would avoid flexing wires but sits further from the dome, which is dimmer but more even. No source tested this; it is a design choice to prototype.
- The 5 V supply from the Lipo Rider suits WS2812s directly. The stock 12 V lamps would be dim or dark at 5 V, but they are being discarded anyway.
- Dome translucency: all are sold as translucent coloured caps (red, yellow, white, green, blue). For player colours driven by RGB, buy **white** (or clear, if offered) caps, because a red cap filters out other colours. This is optics common sense; no source tested colour accuracy through coloured caps.

### Gaps
- No source measured or photographed glow evenness of a WS2812-lit 100 mm dome.
- The internal depth and diameter of a 100 mm cap's underside cavity is not published. Adafruit's "10mm deep plastic base" may describe the bezel, not the cavity.
- No big dome sold with built-in addressable LEDs (WS2812/SK6812) was found. Industrias Lorenzo's RGB buttons are 28 mm and wired for PacLED64 or I-PAC drivers, which suggests analog RGB, not WS2812; this was not confirmed.
